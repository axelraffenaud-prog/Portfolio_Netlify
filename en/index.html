Intervention Model

No off-the-shelf method. An intervention system that adapts.

I do not start with a standard 30/60/90-day plan. I start with the company’s reality, maturity level, urgent priorities, teams and ambition — then adjust the pace and levers as the situation evolves.

01

Observe quickly. Understand accurately. Act where impact is highest.

Field audit, listening to executives and teams, clients and partners, and reviewing data and critical flows. I look for the gap between what the company thinks it should do and what actually creates value.

Étape 01
Understand the context
Rapide • factuel • terrain
I start from the real maturity level, not a theoretical model. Depending on the situation: growth, turnaround, scale-up, exit preparation, integration, internationalization or transformation.
  • Executives, teams, clients, partners, lost prospects
  • P&L, KPIs, data and decision flows
  • Organization, responsibilities and friction points
  • Processes, tools and adoption level
  • Risks, weak signals and immediate levers
Étape 02
Prioritize & secure
Selon l’urgence • sans inertie
I do not try to transform everything. I distinguish between what must be fixed now, what can be industrialized later, and what should not be launched at all.
  • Quick wins à impact immédiat
  • Structural workstreams and dependencies
  • Arbitrages impact / effort / risque
  • Responsibilities, resources and governance
  • Shared indicators and management routines
Étape 03
Transform & accelerate
Itératif • mesurable • évolutif
Once priorities are secured, I evolve the organization in short cycles. Tools, processes and management models change with the company’s maturity — including when new technologies or AI capabilities create a better path.
  • CRM, BI, automation and AI when they create tangible value
  • Agents / copilots to reduce preparatory and repetitive work
  • Management, recruitment and increased autonomy
  • Measure results and reallocate resources
  • Revise the model as field learning accumulates
The real method: continuous adaptation
Contexte→Diagnostic→Priorité→Action→Mesure→Adaptation↺

The approach can be very fast when the company needs to decide quickly, more progressive when buy-in must be secured, or radical when a business-model change demands it. The common foundation remains: clarity, speed of execution, accountability and value creation.

“The biggest risk is that they say yes.” An executive conviction: provoke debate, test assumptions and seek reality before launching a transformation.

Leadership

A leader is not the one who shines alone.
C'est celui qui fait others grow.

As the son and grandson of military men, I inherited a deep sense of duty. I believe in collective intelligence, in each person’s potential, and in the idea that a team progresses when everyone contributes. Leadership means giving purpose and visibility to the people building alongside you.

In executive committees, I favor transparent arbitration, factual data and shared accountability. I do not believe in the lone hero; I believe in an aligned team. This conviction applies both to daily governance and to change management: transforming an organization starts with convincing people to move in the same direction.

A few years ago, a serious injury — a broken ankle, surgery with pins and tendon reconstruction, and a year using a cane — nearly stopped me. Instead of letting it hold me back, I turned it into a strength. Resilience is not declared; it is built. That experience reinforced my conviction that a strong trajectory is built on discipline, clarity and the ability to keep moving when everything seems blocked.

I applied that same conviction to broader communities: founding and leading entrepreneur networks for nearly fifteen years, becoming a point of convergence between projects, people and opportunities.
